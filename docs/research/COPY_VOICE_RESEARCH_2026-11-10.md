# Give the game one coach's voice

_Research report, 2026-11-10. Proposals only: nothing here is scheduled, and no copy in the game has changed. The roadmap is the director's._

**Director ruling, 2026-11-10: option B, the senior coach's briefing.** One coach voice on every screen (no signed staff lines); "we / our" for the club, "you" only for what happens to the coach personally.

## 1. Summary: the voice lives in body lines, not buttons

The game already has firm copy rules (natural AFL language, no engine words, sentence case, Gain and Cost with size and odds at every gate) but **no rule says who is speaking, in which person, or how long a line may be**. So every screen picks its own speaker: break notes say "you", the assistant's report says "us", the feed shouts "we are going to extra time!", event cards slide from tabloid into stat sheet, and tribunal buttons read like a legal form. In a hand sample of 124 lines, **32 carry an engine or software word, 18 are too long, 18 are vague, 17 read like documentation and 16 state a number unclearly**. The awkwardness concentrates in three places: generated gate and event-card cost lines (clauses and parentheses stacked to meet the transparency rule), onboarding and help text, and one action carrying two or three names.

Real AFL coaches write and speak in a register that fixes most of this by itself: 4 to 12 words, one cause per sentence named by area of the ground, past tense for what happened, imperative for the fix, no superlatives, no exclamation marks. Football Manager and Hattrick show the two devices worth borrowing: attribute opinions to a staff member, and anchor every adjective to a fixed, published scale.

Buttons and headers come out the same under every voice: verbs that name the outcome, one name per action. The choice of voice changes body lines, gate lines, break notes and event cards. **Three options for the director to choose from:**

| Option | One-line definition | Person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. The whiteboard | Match-committee notes: subjectless fragments, claim then proof, figures as on a team sheet | "you / your" for the coach, "their" for the opposition |
| B. The senior coach's briefing | The coach's own notes to the group, first-person plural | "we / our" for the club; "you" only for what happens to the coach personally |
| C. The coaches' box | Whiteboard labels and gates (as A), but every assessment is signed by the staff member who made it (assistant, list manager, physio, recruiter); journalists and the commentator keep their own voices | Staff say "we" for the club and "you" to the coach |

## 2. How the game reads now: rules are strong, voice is undefined

### The written rules cover words and numbers, not the speaker

The style guide's copy section asks for what "a coach, recruiter, commentator or supporter would say", no engine words, gates that state "upside, its downside, the size and the odds, in football words and as percentages", "Every line is scrutable" and "Sentence case. Short." (docs/VISUAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md:187-200). Its only voice statement is visual: "the hand-painted scoreboard and signage of a suburban ground" (docs/VISUAL_STYLE_GUIDE.md:18-21). CLAUDE.md repeats the same wide speaker list and adds "player". The afl-ui-writing skill points back to style guide §5 and adds visual rules only. **That wide licence is one root of the mixed register**: five permitted speakers and no rule on which one owns which screen. No document sets person, tense or a length budget for a button, header, gate line or feed line.

### A quarter of the game already sounds like a coach

34 of the 124 lines need no change, and they share one shape: under about 12 words a sentence, an active verb, a football noun, at most one colon joining claim to proof, no parentheses. "Usually %d touches. Tagged, about %d." (L31 GateOdds.gd:17) is the best gate line in the game. "Your midfield is winning the stoppages: clearances %d to %d." (L88) is claim then proof. "You say nothing. The rooms hear it all week anyway." (L56 media.json:118) and "Nothing promised, nothing owed." (L15 HubScene.gd:1781) are the clubroom at its best. "Two seasons short of the board's goals. Your time at %s is over." (L05 HubScene.gd:720) is the blunt verdict the director asked for in press answers. These lines are the style sheet any option should be measured against.

## 3. What other games do: attribution and anchored scales work, repetition kills

### Football Manager gives every opinion a speaker

FM's copy comes from named people (assistant, scout, coach, analyst, journalist), never a neutral system. FM26 merged Home and Inbox into one hub, the Portal, with an action panel for decisions and an "Advice" dropdown where backroom staff speak; Sports Interactive's stated principle is "Overview screens provide you with key general information early and often" ([SI: FM26's reimagined UI](https://footballmanager.com/fm26/features/fm26s-reimagined-user-interface)). **Scout star ratings are explicitly an opinion** relative to your own squad, so the same player rates differently under different staff, and a knowledge percentage shows how sure the report is; at low knowledge the rating shows as a range that narrows ([Passion4FM](https://www.passion4fm.com/football-manager-guide-star-ratings/); [SortItOutSi](https://sortitoutsi.net/content/75753/fm26-how-to-choose-the-best-coach-for-star-ratings-and-reports)). Community guides still warn that a report's words and its stars can disagree.

The mockery is just as consistent. Press conferences have been called repetitive since at least 2014 and were described in 2011 as multiple choice from a faceless interviewer ([WhatCulture](https://whatculture.com/gaming/football-manager-2014-9-issues-it-really-should-fix?page=3); [PC Gamer FM2011](https://www.pcgamer.com/football-manager-2011-review)). FM24 team talks got more situational, naming the actual race a club is in, but reviewers still found stock lines repeating ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/football-manager-2024/review)). Modders have rewritten FM's text commentary for 15 years because the stock lines repeat ([FM Scout pack](https://www.fmscout.com/i-886-The-SOK-Bumper-Commentary-Pack-2010.html)). FM26's tile redesign hid essential facts behind extra clicks; SI admitted some changes were overambitious and rolled them back ([FM Scout](https://www.fmscout.com/a-miles-admits-fm26-missed-the-mark.html); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/football-manager-26-launches-straight-into-a-relegation-battle-as-steam-reviews-plummet-to-mostly-negative-been-playing-since-1993-and-this-is-the-worst-one)).

### Hattrick runs a whole game on a published adjective ladder

Hattrick rates everything with named levels from disastrous to divine on a 0 to 20 scale, each split into four sub-levels, and its help text reasons in the adjectives themselves: a skilled player in bad form can be worse than a lesser one in good form ([Hattrick Match Basics](https://hattrick.org/Help/Rules/MatchBasics.aspx)). **The full scale is printed as an appendix in the rules**, so every word can be looked up ([Hattrick complete rules](https://www.hattrick.org/Help/Rules/Complete.aspx)). This is the condition under which word scales work: fixed order, published, anchored to numbers. It matches the director's Stat Guide ruling ("Words instead") only if the words have a fixed rank.

### OOTP carries story through chained news items

OOTP's storylines are chains of short news articles with a choice point (discipline a player by doing nothing, suspending, trading or releasing him), authored in an XML file users can extend or switch off ([OOTP 13 newsletter](https://www.ootpdevelopments.com/newsletters/nl0094/)). Players split: some want storylines closer to real life, others turn them off for realism ([OOTP forum](https://forums.ootpdevelopments.com/showthread.php?p=3494625)).

### F1 Manager and Madden show the closing loop and its absence

F1 Manager 2022's driver-engineer radio was a reviewer favourite because it answers the player's own instruction, though reviewers wanted more variety; its written press layer is thin by design ([PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/f1-manager-2022-review/); [PC Gamer feature](https://www.pcgamer.com/f1-manager-2022-simulates-the-sport-not-the-press-circus)). Madden's franchise draft keeps being called lacklustre, and a Madden 26 bug that showed every prospect as "top 5" made scouting pointless in players' eyes ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/reviews/madden-27-review/); [EA forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/madden-nfl-26-franchise-en/scouting-bug/12850531)). Madden 20's scouting was praised for a wider spread of ratings ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/review/madden-nfl-20/coming-up-short)). No sourced writing about AFL titles' copy was found; Australian Football Coach advertises a reworked GUI but has no critique of its wording ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2238710)).

### What transfers, and what to avoid

General UX guidance agrees with the director's 3.B.24 ruling: a button names its outcome with a verb, and the same action keeps the same label everywhere ([UX Psychology](https://uxpsychology.substack.com/p/how-to-optimize-button-microcopy)). Five techniques fit this game's rules. **Attribute an opinion to a staff role** so the player weighs the source, as FM does. **Pair a coarse word with a certainty signal**, as FM's knowledge percentage does, which is how the draft's "your recruiters' read of his rating is %d%% tighter" (L104) could read cleanly. **Publish the word ladder** wherever words replace numbers, as Hattrick does. **Echo the coach's call back** in the voice of whoever carries it out, then show the result, as F1 Manager's radio does; this is the bible's principle 3. **Keep the deciding fact on the first layer**, which FM26 learnt the hard way.

Four traps follow from the same sources. Stock lines repeated in weekly scenes (pressers, team talks, feed) are the genre's most mocked fault; fewer scenes with more variants beat many thin ones. A faceless interviewer feels like a form, which this game's named journalists already avoid. Labels that never discriminate ("promising" on everyone) are worthless. And an FM-style "Advice" panel would break the bible outright: staff may state facts and odds, never a pick.

## 4. How real coaches talk: short, collective, cause-first

### Coaches, commentators and journalists write three different languages

AFL.com.au's weekly "What the coaches said" roundups show the senior-coach register plainly: first-person plural, one cause per sentence, named by area of the ground, little colour. Ken Hinkley's line is typical: "When the game was up for grabs, they dominated us in the contest." ([AFL.com.au R3 2025](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1286786/watch-what-the-coaches-said-in-round-three)). In the same roundup Simon Goodwin said his side's method around the ball wasn't where it needed to be "right now", Chris Scott used five words to say his side controlled the start, and Chris Fagan hedged praise with "probably". Commentators work in the opposite register: Bruce McAvaney's signature word recurs so often listeners notice it, and one informal count found 55 rhetorical questions in about 102 minutes of a call ([Boss Hunting](https://bosshunting.com.au/sport/bruce-mcavaney-commentary-analysis)). Journalists headline with conflict verbs and quoted fragments, for example a coach who "slams" a crowd ([AFL.com.au](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1410130/disappointing-craig-mcrae-slams-adelaide-crowd-for-booing-collingwood-magpies-players/amp)).

| Feature | Coach | Commentator | Journalist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Person | we / our; players by first name | he / they; surname or nickname | he / the club; full name, then surname |
| Tense | past for the result, imperative for the fix | present continuous | past, with speculation |
| Sentence length | 4 to 12 words | fragments, exclamations | long, clause-heavy |
| Certainty | hedged ("probably", "right now") | absolute ("special") | speculative ("set to") |
| Technical density | high (contest, stoppage, inside 50) | low to medium | low |
| Questions | rarely | rhetorical, often | in headlines |

Brad Scott's account of the quarter-time break sets the length budget for any break screen: about seven minutes, the last 90 seconds or so for the senior coach, one or two points, and the framing "Right, this is what's happening, this is how we fix it." He names overcomplicating as the biggest mistake ([AFL.com.au, Hot gospel](https://www.afl.com.au/news/84356/hot-gospel-seven-minutes-and-counting)). Coaches'-box messages are imperative and numerical ("an extra number in defence"), drip-fed and aligned before breaks so nobody talks over anybody ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com.au/afl/story/_/id/29192635/from-afl-coach-box-part-2-communication)). Coaches also have clichés to avoid: Footy Almanac's satirical list includes "injuries are no excuse", every game a "must win" and replacements who "earned their spots" ([Footy Almanac](https://www.footyalmanac.com.au/cliches-of-the-press-conference/)).

### The AFL's written formats are the whiteboard the player already knows

**The injury list is three columns: Player, Injury, Estimated return**, with fixed values (Test, TBC, Season, 1 week, 1-2 weeks) ([AFL.com.au Medical room](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1550784/medical-room-the-full-afl-injury-list-r17)). **Team sheets read Ins, Outs, Late changes, Medical sub** ([AFL.com.au final teams](https://www.afl.com.au/news/594695/final-teams-subs-locked-in-for-blues-power-lions-bombers)). **Draft profiles use fixed fields** (state, state-league club, community club, date of birth, height) and then a short paragraph in a set order: positional descriptor, carnival stats, state-league stats, combine testing, background ([AFL.com.au, Sheehan 2026](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1634409/kevin-sheehans-2026-telstra-afl-draft-top-prospects-rucks-and-tall-forwards)). Recruiters state risk as range and confidence ("safest pick", "ceiling") rather than certainty ([RookieMe](https://central.rookieme.com/afl/?p=137773)), and the AFL's draft explainer fixes the list nouns: bid, match the bid, draft points, live trade, father-son, Academy ([AFL.com.au draft explainer](https://www.afl.com.au/news/1253636/afl-draft-explainer-what-is-the-draft-order-compensation-picks-father-son-academies-rookie-pre-season)). Reusing these formats literally on the list, injury, selection and draft screens gives the copy an identity before a single sentence is rewritten.

### Glossary highlights for the shared word list

The game should use the AFL's own nouns: centre bounce (never "ball-up" on a card that also says bounce), stoppage, clearance, hitout, contested possession, inside 50, rebound 50, pressure acts (a real Champion Data term, so L91 can keep it), ball use, corridor, switch, kick-in, flood, numbers behind the ball, spare, tag, lockdown defender, intercept defender, best 22, senior list, rookie list, delist, list spot, trade period, free agency ([Wikipedia glossary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_Australian_rules_football); [AFL junior curriculum](https://play.afl/sites/default/files/2023-10/Junior%20Coaching%20Curriculum%20-%20Guidebook.pdf)). Words to replace: roster (list), playbook (game plan), starter (best 22, or "the player he replaces"), payroll (salary cap), overtime (extra time), franchise (club), probability, success rate, modifier, buff, unlock. Spelling: defence, centre, metres, colour, program, practise (verb).

## 5. Three voices, side by side on the same 31 lines

Every option keeps the same five bible guarantees: Gain and Cost at every gate with size and odds as percentages inside the line (rulings 3.B.27 and Q39); no line says or implies which option is better; football nouns from the glossary; Australian English; and, for readability, one fact per line with structure (line breaks, a meter) doing the work that clauses and parentheses do today.

### Rules common to all three options

**Buttons** are 1 to 4 words, a verb naming the outcome, identical to the words the body uses ("Stack the centre bounce", never a second name). **Headers** are 1 to 4 words, a noun or place, no verb, no full stop. **Gate lines** follow one grammar: `Gain: <one effect, one number>` and `Cost: <one effect, one number>`, one unit per line, a second effect on its own line rather than after a comma. **Hidden scales** never appear raw: a 0-100 meter reads "%d out of 100" and keeps its name. **Banned everywhere**: should, recommend, best (as a verdict), need to, consider, try, probability, chance of success, modifier, simulation, Auto-pick and other feature names, roster, starter, payroll, exclamation marks outside the broadcaster's feed and quoted commentary, parentheses in gate lines. **Humour** follows ruling 3.B.34 unchanged.

### Option A, the whiteboard

Copy reads like the match committee's sheet. **Sentences 3 to 10 words**, often subjectless fragments ("Plays sore."). Person: "you / your" for the coach, as the ruled examples already do ("their shots 25% more accurate"). Results in past tense, instructions as bare imperatives. Odds as `<effect> <n>%` ("Their shots 25% more accurate"). Break notes: at most two points, claim then proof with one colon. Feed: neutral match-report fragments ("Free kick to %s"). Help text: one fact per line, no "This is where". **Fits the bible** most easily: fragments leave no room for advice, and "you" matches every existing ruled wording (3.B.36). **Risk**: it can turn cold and telegraphic, losing the "clubroom personality" in the 3.B.34 guidance voice; event-card scene-setting (L52, L56) should keep its two-beat prose.

### Option B, the senior coach's briefing

Copy reads like the coach's own notes to the group. **Sentences 4 to 12 words.** Person: "we / our / us" for the club and players; **"you" only for what happens to the coach personally** (sacked, a player's opinion of you, a promise you made), because "the board sacks us" or "his opinion of us" says something false. Past tense for results, imperative for fixes, "right now" over "always". Odds in the same percentage form as A, with "we" as subject where one is needed ("We win 18% more centre clearances"). Feed: a broadcaster's voice, set apart from the coach's (director, 2026-11-10: "Keep broadcaster"), since the coach does not call the game. **Fits the bible** with care: "we need to" and "we should" drift straight into advice, so they join the banned list. **Strength**: it is the register real coaches use and it reads as an identity at once. **Risk**: the we/you split is a rule every writer must apply exactly, and gates on the opposition's plays read slightly odd in first person.

### Option C, the coaches' box

Labels, help text and gates are written exactly as A. **Assessments are signed by the staff member who made them**: the assistant at breaks and in the post-match report, the list manager on contracts and draft needs, the recruiter on prospects, the physio on injuries, the development coach on training. Staff say "we" for the club and "you" to the coach, as FM's staff and real assistants do. Journalists keep their personalities in press and news, and the commentator keeps a broadcaster's voice in the feed, visually set apart. **Fits the bible** only with two guards: staff state facts and odds, never a pick (an FM-style "Advice" panel is out), and ruling 3.B.34 says "no new dialogue/news framework", so signed staff lines need the director's explicit go-ahead. **Strength**: different voices for different jobs, the player weighs the source, and the press, feed and cards stop clashing because each has an owner. **Risk**: the most to write and the most to keep consistent; it also adds a speaker label to lines that are short today.
